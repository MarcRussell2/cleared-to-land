THE DIRECTION, fixed by the owner: real and atmospheric - a flight simulator out of a real window, not a cartoon, not neon, not "cinematic". The spirit is old Hong Kong and the Kai Tak approach: a dense harbor city of concrete apartment blocks with air-conditioners and water tanks on every roof, a downtown of glass office towers, a suspension bridge across the bay, cranes and container stacks on an island, and a famous orange-and-white checkerboard painted on a hill that pilots aimed at before a late turn onto the runway. By day: haze, sun on weathered concrete, glass reflecting the sky. By night: thousands of lit windows in warm and cool whites, sodium-orange streets, red obstacle lights on every tall roof, the bridge's lamps in a line across the black water, the checkerboard floodlit.

WHAT METRO CITY IS (you cannot see it, so here it is in words)
The runway is 3,000 m long and runs north along the waterfront, on a level strip of reclaimed land with the airport buildings on its east side and the harbor 600 m to its right. Airliners land northbound, so they approach from the south, and the six missions ("The city" ladder) are six ways of doing that:
- Short final (the last 4 km south of the runway) is over land: apartment blocks, mostly 20-45 m tall, packed on a street grid right up to the extended runway centerline, kept 30 m under the glide path. West of them the old town rises to 110 m; south-west, on the bay's shore 4.5-5.5 km out, downtown: office towers to 280 m.
- Beyond 4 km the approach crosses a bay: water under the centerline from 4 to 7.7 km out, the west shore 160-340 m to the left, then land and steep hills again beyond.
- Checkerboard Hill: a 110 m hill 900 m left of short final and 700 m out, grass and rock, with a 110 x 90 m checkerboard board (11 x 9 squares of 10 m) lying on its south-east flank, facing an airliner that comes in from the south-east over the water. The pilot flies straight at the board and turns right 45 degrees two kilometres out, low over the rooftops: that turn is the Kai Tak moment, and the board must read at 3-5 km in haze and at night (it is floodlit).
- The Harbor Bridge: a suspension bridge 1,442 m long crossing the bay 5.7 km out, from the west shore to Container Island. The deck is 60 m over the water (the Under the Bridge mission flies beneath it at 30 m); two 150 m towers stand at 42% of the length either side of the middle; the main cables sag from the tower tops to the deck at mid-span; hangers every 18 m; anchorages at both ends; sodium lamps along both deck edges every 60 m.
- Container Island: a 750 x 700 m concrete quay in the bay east of the approach, with container yards, three ship-to-shore cranes (booms raised) and two warehouses.
- Missions add their own: Grand Avenue (Downtown, the Gauntlet): office towers taller than the glide path along both sides of a 120 m boulevard straight down the centerline for the last 3 km, with glass skybridges across it that the airliner must fly under; the Slalom: pairs of skyscrapers across the final with a 56 m gap in each, 60 m left and right of the centerline in turn; the Needle: two slender round glass towers on a pier in the bay, 34 m apart and joined at the top by a skybridge, flown through banked 30-40 degrees.

WHAT YOU DRAW, and from what (the header of city-look.js has every field)
Everything solid arrives as a list of collision volumes ("prims"): oriented boxes, vertical cylinders and frustums, and capsules, each with a `kind` (what it is), a `look` (how the plain look draws it), a `name`, a `group` (all the parts of one building, crane or bridge share it), sometimes a palette `color`, and a `hint` - the look hints the missions and districts set so you never need to read the world code:
- buildings (kind tower / block): hint.cls residential | office | industrial | landmark | skybridge; hint.facade concrete (flats) | glass (offices) | shed (warehouses); hint.roof plant | crown | flat | spire; hint.lit, the fraction of windows lit at night; hint.height low (< 25 m) | mid (< 60) | high (< 150) | super. About 4,500 of them, all boxes except the Needle's two towers (cylinders).
- skybridges (kind skybridge): boxes spanning a street, glass, lit; red lights mark their undersides' ends.
- Checkerboard Hill (kind landmark): a frustum (hint.part 'hill') and a thin box lying on its flank (hint.part 'board', hint.squares [11, 9], across and up; the plain look paints 10 m squares on the box's two broad faces, its local Y faces).
- the bridge (kinds bridge-deck, bridge-pier, bridge-tower (with hint.part 'portal' for the beam across the top), bridge-anchor, cable (hint.part 'main-cable' or 'hanger')).
- cranes, container stacks, the quays, ships, masts, pylons and wires, spruce: the obstacle missions' kinds, already drawn well - keep them at least as good (the three "In the way" missions use the same file).
- the ground (`ground` areas, never solid): a district's street grid (kind 'city', with the grid's origin, axes, block pitch and street width), 'avenue' (the boulevard down lp.y = 0 of its area), 'plaza', 'apron' (the island's quay top, at a fixed height). The plain look drapes one mesh 0.3 m over the terrain, skipping water and cells already covered.
- gates: markers (orange and white frames) the missions fly through, never solid; lights: red obstacle lights (the tall ones blink), white floodlights, amber bridge lamps.

THE RULES THAT MATTER MOST (tools/test-obstacles.mjs sections 4 and 10 enforce them; run `node tools/test-obstacles.mjs`)
1. What is drawn solid is what collides. Draw each prim from its own numbers. You may add detail inside a volume (setbacks, balconies, rooftop plant, water tanks, a crown, a spire, window reveals, cladding ribs) within 0.5 m + 2% of the side, and the drawing of each prim must reach every face of it within the same tolerance - an airplane that clears what it sees clears the volume, and one that hits what it sees hits it. Nothing solid may be drawn outside its volume (no antennas, signs or balconies sticking out: the masts that exist are prims of their own). The trees stay the world's spruce model.
2. Say which prim you draw: instanced meshes carry userData.prim (the prim of each instance, -1 for markers); a merged mesh carries a vertex attribute `ctPrim`. Decoration that is not solid (the ground and what is painted on it) sets userData.decor = true and lies on the ground (within 1.5 m of the terrain); anything that stands up (a lamp post, a sign) is solid, so it can only be drawn inside a prim, and lamps at night are light points.
3. The check reads your GEOMETRY, and so does the shadow pass (three draws every caster with its own depth material: your onBeforeCompile never runs there). Vertex-shader deformation is invisible to both (only the plain look's ctTaper on thin members is emulated by the check), so build shapes as geometry, not by moving vertices in a shader: the plain look builds each frustum among the round buildings and the hill as its own tapered geometry for exactly that reason (a 170 m hill cast the shadow of a 170 m cylinder).
4. Keep the two shadow primers exactly as they are (they stop a shadow shader compiling mid-approach). Nothing may be created after buildCourse returns.

WHAT TO MAKE OF IT (ideas, not orders; the plain look is fourteen facade and surface styles in one shader):
- flats: stained concrete and tile, small windows with air-conditioner boxes and balcony slabs, laundry-coloured noise, rooftop water tanks and plant rooms (inside the volume), a darker ground floor of shops with signs as colour (not geometry sticking out);
- offices: curtain walls with mullions, spandrels, a lit crown at night, reflections that fade to haze colour at range; the Needle: a finer glass, a spire (its antenna is a separate mast prim), lit bands at night;
- the bridge: grey steel towers with portal beams, cables that stay visible as thin dark lines at range (the plain look widens thin members to a pixel in the vertex shader: that is allowed, the geometry stays the true radius), a deck edge with railings and the lamp line;
- the hill: grass and scrub with rock outcrops, the board as painted panels on a frame, floodlit at night;
- the ground: asphalt streets with markings, pavements, plazas; at night a sodium glow along the streets and the avenue, never brighter than the lamps themselves; the far city at night a carpet of warm points, not a white sheet;
- radiance: sunlit white is about 0.8 linear, the bloom thresholds at 0.92, only lamps and glints above 1; lit windows are lamps, but a whole facade of them must not bloom into a white wall (today's windows are 0.45 at most, and the facade fades to their average where they get finer than a pixel).
