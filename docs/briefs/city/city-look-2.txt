WHAT TO FIX, most important first:
1. The far city at night is now black. From 2-5 km at night (the Gauntlet's opening view, the Checkerboard approach at 21:36) the districts are black boxes with red roof lights and nothing else: the windows fade to zero where they get finer than a pixel (aa * aa), so beyond a few hundred metres there are no lit windows at all. The owner's brief asks for "thousands of lit windows ... the far city at night a carpet of warm points, not a white sheet". Wanted: lit windows that stay visible as scattered points at range without an even glow and without crawling. One way (ideas, not orders): a level of detail for the window hash - when a window cell drops under about two pixels, switch to super-cells of 2x2, 4x4, 8x8 windows chosen by the pixel footprint (blend between two levels), each super-cell lit or not by an integer hash of its own index with a probability and a brightness chosen so the facade's average stays about the same as the windows it stands for; the result stays a stable speckle of points down to the horizon. Keep the average low: the old "tan wall" was an even 0.1 over every facade, and the night exposure multiplies everything (sky.js: renderer.toneMappingExposure = lerp(3.8, 1.24, dayness) - an emission of 0.065 at night displays like 0.25 by day). An average of 0.01-0.02 per facade with the lit points well above it is the target; the old red roof lights and the bridge lamps must still stand out.
2. Dusk: the engine's night flag is on from 19:30 (main.js: night = time < 6.5 || time > 19.5), while the sky is still bright twilight. The tan wall of the plain look came from its EMISSION, not from the albedo; the night's lighting and exposure already make a facade dark. Remove the albedo darkening by the night flag (or keep it very mild), so the buildings at dusk keep their colours dimly and the lit windows come up over them; at full night the facades are then as dark as the terrain around them, which is right.
