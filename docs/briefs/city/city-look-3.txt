WHY THE TOWERS ARE BLANK BY DAY (read this before item 1)
From the chase camera (55 degree vertical field of view; the owner's stills are 1600x900) a 3.6 m storey is about 110 px tall at 30 m, 17 px at 200 m and 3-4 px at 1 km; a 1 m spandrel is 30, 5 and 1 px; a 7 cm mullion is 2 px at 30 m and a third of a pixel at 200 m. On the faces the camera sees most (Grand Avenue's canyon walls, the Slalom's faces 20-60 m off the wingtip) the view is grazing, so the bays are squeezed sideways far more than that.
Three things in today's code make the glass a blank pale slab:
- The sky sheen. The emissivemap step in city-look.js adds `ctGloss * (1 - ctNight) * mix(0.12, 0.62, F) * vec3(0.30, 0.40, 0.52)` to every glass pixel. At the grazing angles of the canyon F is near 1, so that is about 0.25 linear of one flat blue-grey over the whole face - more than the diffuse light the dark glass albedo gives back. The spandrels and mullions only darken the ALBEDO (by 10-18% and 30%) and keep nearly the full gloss, so the sheen paints straight over them. Structure has to modulate the reflection too: an opaque spandrel panel and a metal mullion reflect far less than vision glass (give them low gloss, or their own darker reflection), and the reflection itself should not be one flat colour.
- The lines fade to nothing. Each mullion and floor line is multiplied by (1 - smoothstep(0.5, 1.5, fw / lineWidth)), which is zero wherever the line is under a pixel wide - everywhere the camera ever is. A line should fade to its AREA-WEIGHTED AVERAGE instead (a box-filtered line: integrate its coverage over the pixel footprint, so a 1 m spandrel in a 3.6 m storey at 60% contrast tends to an even 17% darkening when the storey is under a pixel, and is a crisp band when it is several pixels). That keeps pass 2's freedom from moire, because a filtered line never aliases, and it keeps the structure visible.
- The blocky tone. `glass * (0.86 + 0.22 * variation)` gives every 1.8 x 3.6 m panel its own step of up to 25%: close up it reads as a pixelated texture, and at range it is noise without structure.
