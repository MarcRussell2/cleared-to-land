WHAT TO BUILD

1. world-atmosphere.js - a physically motivated sky.
   Single-scattering Rayleigh + Mie, baked on the CPU into a small lookup texture at SkySystem.set(time, visibility): for example a 128x64 RGB DataTexture indexed by (cosine of the angle to the sun, view elevation) - or whatever parametrisation you prefer - plus a second small table or a few uniforms for the horizon haze tied to visibility and for the night sky. atmosphere(d) becomes one or two texture fetches and a handful of instructions. It runs per fragment on EVERY fog-enabled surface in the scene as the aerial perspective (SkySystem.installFog injects it into every material), so no per-pixel pow chains and no noise. What it must show: the sun-side brightening (forward Mie scatter), a darker and more saturated zenith, a haze band on the horizon that thickens as visibility drops, a warm low sun, the blue hour, and a night that is not black: a moonlit sky is a dark blue-grey, brightest toward the moon, with a faintly lighter band at the horizon so the horizon can always be found.
   Keep celestial(d): a proper sun disc (0.53 degrees across) with a glare/corona whose size follows the haze, and the moon as a disc with a soft glow. Both stay added by the dome only.
   - KEEP every uniform that exists today - atSun, atMoon, atZenith, atHorizon, atWarm, atNight, atDay, atLow, atWeather, atExtinction - declared in atmosphereGLSL / returned by atmosphereUniforms(), and keep them MEANINGFUL: world-water.js uses atSun, atDay, atLow, atWeather, atWarm and atMoon in its own shader code, and sky.js's installFog reads atDay, atWeather and atExtinction. Your new uniforms start with `at` (atLut, atLutNight, atHaze ...). A sampler uniform is a uniform like any other; because installFog() merges `this.uniforms` into every fogged material, a lookup texture must exist (created, with data) in the constructor, before the first update().
   - Keep the exported names: atmosphereGLSL, atmosphereUniforms(), worldVertex, outputGLSL, VISIBILITY_EXTINCTION. Keep the signatures `vec3 atmosphere(vec3 d)` and `vec3 celestial(vec3 d)`.

2. sky.js - the light, the exposure, the fog, the clouds, the stars and the moon.
   THE RADIANCE BUDGET (this is the single most important change): the composer renders the scene into a linear HDR render target WITHOUT tone mapping (three.js skips tone mapping whenever a render target is bound), the UnrealBloomPass then thresholds that linear radiance at 0.92 (engine, fixed: strength 0.35, radius 0.5), and only the OutputPass applies ACES with renderer.toneMappingExposure. Three r169 lights are physically based: a Lambert surface's outgoing radiance is (sum of the lights' irradiance) x albedo / pi, so today's noon sun (5.2) plus fill (1.1) puts a white surface at about 1.8 linear - twice the bloom threshold - and the exposure of 0.5 hides it by flattening the whole picture. Instead: a sunlit white surface (albedo 0.9, facing the sun) lands at about 0.8 linear before tone mapping (sun about 2.3 and fill about 0.5 at noon, scaled down at low sun and in weather); only the sun disc, the aerodrome and deck lights, the strobes and real specular glints may exceed 1.0; renderer.toneMappingExposure is then set per time and visibility so the picture reads right at that budget - around 1.0-1.3 by day, higher at dusk and night. With the composer off (the low tier renders straight to the screen) the same shaders tone-map in place and the result must match. This one change stops the markings glowing without touching the aerodrome.
   - Key and fill: sunlight strong and directional; the hemisphere fill is the sky's own colour from above (zenith-ish) and a ground-albedo bounce from below, and lower than today, so that shadows are visibly bluer and the sun's angle can be read on every surface. Sun colour from the same scattering model as the sky: white at noon, warm at low elevation, deep orange at 7-8 degrees.
   - Keep the sun elevation model (e = sin((time - 6.5) / 13 * pi) * 62 degrees) and the azimuth the engine passes in: the shadow directions and the review scenes depend on them. Times run 07:00 to 22:30 (dayness fades over -6..+6 degrees of elevation); both ends must look finished.
   - Aerial perspective: keep the installFog() mechanism exactly as it is (the shader chunk replacement, the customProgramCacheKey bump - and bump its version string when you change the injected code). Fix its strength: use Koschmieder, contrast = exp(-3.912 * distance / visibility), so that at 25-40 km visibility the ground 1.5 km away keeps most of its contrast while hills 10-15 km away turn blue-grey and melt into the haze band. The in-scattered colour comes from the LUT for the view direction, so it is brighter toward the sun. Fog scenes (700 m): the fog is brighter above than below (lit from the sky), faintly warm toward the sun even when the sun cannot be seen, objects fade into it with the correct exponential, and the horizon glow is gone. Keep the exponential path for dense weather.
   - Clouds: keep the layered-sheet approach (two, at most three planes) but give them form: a baked cumulus density map with soft-edged puffs and clear gaps, lit by the sun with a darker underside and a bright rim toward the sun (use the density gradient you already bake into the texture), bluer with distance, fading out at the far edge; a thin high cirrus veil is fine as the third layer. No per-pixel noise. Coverage comes from opts.cloudCover (0.3 by default; 0 in fog); the base height from opts.elevation. At dusk the clouds take the sunset light; at night they are dark shapes against the moonlit sky.
   - Stars: keep them, but fade them by the sky's own brightness (not just by dayness) so they show only where the sky is dark, and hide them in the horizon haze.
   - Moon: a fixed position is fine, but its light (this.moon, a DirectionalLight) must be strong enough, with the night exposure, that the sea, the ground and the carrier's deck are visible at 22:30 - a night you can see into, not a day with a blue filter, and not black. Cool colour.
   - The menu backdrop (plains, 08:30, 30 km, low camera down the runway): inviting - warm low sun, long soft shadows, light haze.
   - Keep the SkySystem API exactly: the constructor options (time, visibility, azimuth, elevation, cloudCover, shadowSize, detail), set(time, visibility), update(aircraftPos, dt), setShadowSize(n), and the fields sunDir, sunElev, dayness, sun, fogColor, uniforms, and installFog(). The constructor's setWorldQuality() call stays first.
