WHAT TO BUILD

1. Tree models, low-poly but shaped. Per type, three levels of detail, each one merged geometry (use mergeGeos from ../geom.js) with vertex colours or a per-instance colour:
   - LOD0 (within ~350 m on high): a conifer with a tapered trunk and 5-7 tiers of branch whorls made of a few cross-planes or cones, irregular, about 200-300 triangles; a broadleaf with a trunk, two or three main limbs and a crown of 6-9 overlapping ellipsoid lobes at different heights and offsets, about 200-300 triangles. Alpha-tested cross-plane foliage cards drawn into a CanvasTexture (branch silhouettes, no image files) are welcome and cheap - they cast good shadows.
   - LOD1 (350 m to ~1.5 km): the same silhouette in 40-70 triangles.
   - LOD2 (beyond): a two-quad cross billboard or a single camera-facing quad from a baked silhouette texture (draw the tree's silhouette with lit/shaded halves into a 128x128 canvas; a cheap vertex shader rotates the quad to face the camera), 2-4 triangles.
   Two conifer species (spruce: narrow, dark, drooping tiers; pine: taller trunk, flatter crown) and two broadleaf shapes (round oak-like; taller poplar-like), each with hue/lightness variation per instance from PALETTE.trees (keep the existing keys conifer/broadleaf/obstacle and add what you need). Sizes vary: 8-22 m broadleaf, 10-28 m conifer, smaller near the treeline.

2. Placement and instancing. Keep the deterministic single-rng walk (the header explains why: lowering treeScale removes trees rather than moving the survivors). Draw calls for a typical view on high: under 40 for all vegetation. castShadow: only LOD0 meshes cast (the shadow camera is a 180 m box around the airplane; LOD1/LOD2 never fall inside it usefully); receiveShadow off. Triangle budget for the plains on high: under 500k for all vegetation in a typical view (the probe counts the shadow pass too); mountain sites under 700k. Trees per site may stay at the current counts if the LODs bring the triangles down; reduce counts by treeScale as today.
   Forest structure: woodland from field.forestNoise (keep the thresholds so the ground's woodland floor matches: a tree stands where the floor is dark), with a ragged margin (single trees and small groups scattered 30-80 m outside the edge, thinning with distance), the occasional clearing inside, species mix by site (plains: mixed broadleaf with a few pines; coast: more pine and wind-bent shapes near the shore; mountain: spruce almost everywhere, larch-like paler conifers near the treeline, broadleaf only on the valley floor along the river), density thinning toward the treeline (field.snowLine - 280 m or so, jittered) and nothing above it.

3. Hedgerows as hedges. Along parcel edges on the plains and coast (parcel(px,pz).edge < 8, low ground, not woodland, not on a flat): a continuous hedge, not a row of trees. Build one hedge-segment geometry (a lumpy box about 2 m high x 3 m deep with a rounded, uneven top, 20-40 triangles, vertex-coloured dark green with a lighter top) and instance it end to end along the parcel edges with the edge's `angle`, with gaps at the field gates every 100-200 m, and an occasional real broadleaf tree standing in the hedge line (every 60-120 m, seeded). Under THREE.LOD like the trees: beyond ~2 km the hedge instances drop out (the ground's parcel borders carry the line at that distance). Keep hedges off the runway flats (field.nearFlat with a margin).

4. Obstacle trees (buildObstacleTrees): the spruce wall and the singles across the bush approaches. Use the LOD0 spruce, one Mesh per entry as now (they are few), scaled by t.scale, seeded rotation. OBSTACLE_TREE { radius, height } is the collision cylinder at scale 1: if the new spruce is a different size, change the two numbers with it so the collision matches the picture. Trunk visibly bare for the lower third so the pilot can judge height against the wall.
