WHAT TO BUILD

1. A chunked, level-of-detail terrain mesh. buildGround(field, opts) returns a THREE.Group named 'world/ground'. Divide the field.size square into chunks (for example 8x8 chunks of 3.25 km on a 26 km field; pick what works) and put each chunk under a THREE.LOD with two or three levels (the renderer calls LOD.update(camera) itself every frame; nothing else in the engine will call you). Suggested spacing on high: about 20 m near (within ~2.5 km of the chunk), 60-80 m mid, 240 m far; on medium 30/100/300; on low 40/160/480 and only two levels. Add skirts (a vertical strip hanging down a few metres from each chunk edge) so that neighbouring chunks at different levels never show cracks - do not try to match edges. Every mesh gets a bounding sphere (computeBoundingSphere) so frustum culling works; nothing here is frustumCulled = false. receiveShadow on every level; castShadow off. The sum for a typical view on high must stay under about 400k triangles and 25 draw calls for the ground alone; report your numbers.
   The runway sits on a "flat" (field.nearFlat / the flats built into field.height): keep the near sampling dense enough (20 m or finer) around the flats that the runway's own quad does not float above or sink into the ground.

2. The ground colouring. Rewrite groundColor(field, h, slope, x, z, out) - keep the signature and export (the barrel and the test read it) - as a proper layered material by site style:
   - plains/coast: farmland (the parcel system) on flat low ground, meadow/pasture on gentle slopes and around woodland (field.forestNoise), darker damp ground in hollows, bare earth on the steepest bits; the woodland floor under forests (forestNoise > 0.28) is a darker, browner green.
   - coast: dry sand above the waterline, wet darker sand in the last 1.5 m to the water, shelving into the water colour over a few metres, dune grass above; the far hills as on the plains.
   - mountain: NO farm parcels and NO ploughed/pasture blotches. Valley floor: meadow greens with gravel banks along the river (field.riverAxis), wet sand right at the water; slopes: grass to alpine turf by altitude, rock where the slope is steep (use the normal), scree fans below rock, the snow line from field.snowLine with wind-scoured rock showing on steep faces above it and snow pockets below it on shallow ground; the treeline band (field.snowLine - 280 m or so, the vegetation module uses this) slightly paler.
   - sea: not built (the site is water).
   Colours come from PALETTE.ground: keep every key that exists (the contract test checks them) and add what you need.

3. The ground material and its textures. One MeshStandardMaterial (name 'world/ground') shared by every chunk and level, vertexColors on, with an onBeforeCompile pass (customProgramCacheKey 'ctl-ground-v2' or higher) that does, per fragment and cheaply:
   - the farm parcels as today (parcel(x, z) stays the shared grid - world-vegetation.js places hedges on its edges - but you may change the border look): one seeded crop colour per parcel from a 64x64 nearest-filtered lookup, hard borders, faint crop rows, and now: per-parcel variation (a second lookup channel for "bare / stubble / green / tall"), a thin bare-earth headland strip inside the border, occasional tractor tracks, and a soft wet/dry gradient across the parcel.
   - the detail texture layered at two scales (near ~8 m tile for the flare, far ~90 m) with the near layer fading out beyond ~400 m so distant ground does not turn into a uniform tile. Rebuild groundDetail(seed) in textures.js as a REAL ground texture: a 1024 px periodic canvas with clumped grass, small bare patches, tiny stones and shadowed tufts, mid-grey average (so vertex colours are the albedo; drop detailCompensation if you can, but keep the key), tinted per site by a uniform, not by the texture.
   - a subtle normal/relief cue: a cheap slope-lit term from the detail texture's luminance gradient, so the ground near the airplane has visible micro-relief in low sun (a few instructions; no normal map texture required).
   - a faint distance-based desaturation is NOT yours: the atmosphere already does aerial perspective. Do not add fog or haze here.
   Keep the material fog-enabled (fog: true, the default) so installFog() gives it the aerial perspective; do not declare any uniform starting with `at`; prefix yours `gr`.
